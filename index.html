Stages

Numbered as in the RIBA Plan of Work, from the brief to the park in use.

  1. 0

    Strategic definition

    The site, the business case and whether the project should go ahead.

    Site visit, Business case, Constraints, Decision to proceed.

  2. 1

    Preparation and briefing

    The brief, the surveys, the budget and the programme.

    Brief, Measured survey, Condition survey, Guest data, Budget, Programme.

  3. 2

    Concept design

    Layout, guest route, theme and capacity, and the outline planning position.

    Concept design, Guest route, Capacity, Theme, Outline cost.

  4. 3

    Spatial coordination

    The design coordinated with structure, services, the ride and the theming. Planning application where it is required.

    Coordinated design, Planning application, Ride coordination, Cost plan.

  5. 4

    Technical design

    The information for construction, repair, making and theming.

    Technical drawings, Specification, Construction information.

  6. 5

    Manufacturing and construction

    Construction, scenic making and theming, phased around the season.

    Construction, Scenic manufacture, Theming, Installation.

  7. 6

    Handover

    The finished work handed to the operator, with the information to run and maintain it.

    Handover, Operating information, Defects.

  8. 7

    Use

    The park in operation. The result checked against the brief, and the next season planned.

    In-use review, Maintenance, Next season.

Common issues

All services

Each issue links to the service we use for it.

  • A new attraction is being planned

    A new ride or land needs a building, a queue and a setting designed around the guest.

    New Attractions and Expansions

  • Parts of the site are tired

    Theming has worn, or an area is passed through rather than used.

    Refurbishment and Retheming

  • Queues dominate guest feedback

    A long queue with little shade and no published wait reduces throughput and the rest of the visit.

    Guest Experience Review

  • New work needs permission

    A new structure, a change of use, or growth on a constrained site.

    Masterplanning and Planning Applications

  • The condition of the site is unclear

    Buildings, shelters and theming age, and drawings are often missing or out of date.

    Building and Condition Surveys

  • Not every guest can use the whole site

    Routes, entrances, toilets and queues that some guests cannot use.

    Accessibility Reviews

The work

Design, repair, planning, construction and theming, as well as the review and the survey that define the problem.

  • New Attractions and Expansions

    Ride buildings, themed lands, queue buildings and entrances, designed around how guests will use them.

    4 to 12 months, by stage

  • Refurbishment and Retheming

    Existing areas, queues and buildings improved without closing the attraction.

    8 to 20 weeks

  • Masterplanning and Planning Applications

    A long-term plan for the site, and the planning permission to deliver it.

    3 to 4 months, plus the planning period

How we work

  • On site

    The project starts with a walk of the park and a clear brief.

  • Design

    Buildings, queues, lands and the changes to what is already there.

  • Delivery

    Planning, repair, construction, making and theming, through to the finished work.

  • One team

    The same appointment can cover the problem, the design and the work on site.
