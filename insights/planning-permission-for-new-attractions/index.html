Planning permission for new attractions: what operators should know

The questions a council usually asks, and why the application should describe the whole site.

The usual council questions

A new ride, land or building is rarely judged on the attraction alone. Councils ask about height, noise, traffic, landscape, heritage and whether the site sits in the green belt.

Those questions are easier to answer if they are asked before the design is fixed. A drawing that ignores them tends to come back.

Pre-application advice

A pre-application discussion with the council is a way to test the proposal before a full application is made. It is not a decision, and it is not a guarantee.

It is useful when the site has constraints, or when the operator has not made a planning application for some years.

The timetable

In England, a planning application is usually decided in 8 weeks, or 13 weeks if it is classed as a major application. Those periods can extend if the council asks for more information.

The time before submission is often longer than the statutory period. Surveys, drawings and specialist reports have to exist first.

Planning the whole site

An application for one building is simpler if it sits inside a plan for the site. Routes, parking, servicing and later phases should be visible, even when only one phase is being applied for.

A masterplan is the document that holds that sequence. The application is the part of it that needs permission now.

This article is general guidance for England. Rules differ in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, and every site is different. It is not legal advice.
