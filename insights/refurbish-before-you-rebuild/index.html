Insights

25 September 2026

Refurbish before you rebuild

A tired area is not always a reason for a new building. Start with what is already there.

Start with a survey

Before a new building is drawn, the condition of what is already there should be known. Many parks do not have accurate drawings of shelters, queue structures or themed facades.

A measured and condition survey sets out what can stay, what needs repair, and what should be replaced. That order is cheaper to establish in winter than after a design has been commissioned.

Change the experience, not only the surface

A new coat of paint does not change a queue that has no shade, no information and nowhere to lean. Guests notice the wait, the route and the places they stop.

Refurbishment should be tested against the visit: what people do in that space, how long they stay, and whether the area is used or only passed through.

Phase around the season

Most of this work can be done while the attraction stays open, if it is sequenced. Surveys sit outside operating hours. Building work is phased around the closed season and the quieter weeks.

A plan that assumes a long shutdown is often the plan that does not get built.

The carbon case

Keeping a structure and improving how it performs usually uses less material than demolishing it. Shade, shelter and planting can do part of the work that a new roof would otherwise be asked to do.

This is an approach, not a certification. Where a formal carbon assessment is required, that is a specialist appointment.
