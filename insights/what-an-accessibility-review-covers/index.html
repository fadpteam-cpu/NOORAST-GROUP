What an accessibility review covers

A design review of the visit, from before arrival to the routes around the site. It is not a certified audit.

Before the visit

Guests decide whether they can come before they arrive. The website, the map and the information about parking, toilets and rides are part of the review.

If that information is missing or out of date, the visit has already failed for some people.

Arrival and entrance

We look at drop-off, parking, the walk to the gate, ticket desks and the entrance itself. Gradients, surfaces, gates and queues are where many visits become difficult.

A step that most guests do not notice can decide the day for a wheelchair user, a buggy or an older visitor.

Around the site

Routes, resting points, toilets, changing places, food, viewing areas and the queues themselves. The review follows the paths guests actually take, not only the ones on the map.

Recommendations are set out in order, from a sign or a handrail to work that needs design.

What you receive

A report with photographs, a list of changes in order of priority, and draft access information for the website.

This is a design review, not a certified access audit. Where a formal audit is needed, we will say so and can bring in a registered access consultant.
